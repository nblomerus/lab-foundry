# [Article title]

> Decisive evidence reads as a PAPER; mixed/inconclusive evidence reads as an honest RESEARCH NOTE
> that says exactly what was and was not settled. Either way: every number is one the lab actually
> measured (never invent or embellish), and every prior-work claim cites an EXACT corpus title.

## Abstract
[150-300 words: the question, what was done, the headline result with its key number, and the honest
verdict (supported / refuted / mixed / inconclusive).]

## 1. Introduction
[The problem, why it matters, the direction's claim, and the contribution this article makes.]

## 2. Related Work
[From the literature review — each prior-work claim cites an EXACT corpus title. Position this work
against them: what gap it addresses.]

## 3. Method
[What was actually built/run across the experiment series — models, the dataset(s) used (name the
real /data dataset where one was used), procedure, controls. Be concrete and reproducible.]

## 4. Experiments
[The experiment series, per hypothesis (H1, H2, …): what each tested and on what data. State the
data realism plainly — REAL dataset vs synthetic/builtin — since it bounds what the result can claim.]

## 5. Results
[The REAL measured numbers, per hypothesis: metric, effect/delta, comparison to baseline, and whether
each hypothesis's decision threshold was met. Tables/numbers only — no vague "improved".]

## 6. Discussion
[What the numbers mean for the direction's claim. Be honest about strength of evidence; if synthetic-
only, say the claim is not yet established on real data and a real-data confirmation is needed.]

## 7. Limitations
[Honest scope: synthetic/toy data, single GPU, small N, confounds, what was NOT shown.]

## 8. Reproducibility
[Seeds, code hashes, image digests from the experiments' provenance; the datasets + slices used.]

## 9. Conclusion
[The one-sentence defensible takeaway and the most useful next step.]
